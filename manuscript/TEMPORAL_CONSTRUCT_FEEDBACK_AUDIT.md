# Temporal Construct Feedback Audit (Teammate Feedback Item 7)

**Feedback:** "Answer position may influence temporal behavior; determine
whether the current counterbalancing and construct-validity language is
sufficient; do not strengthen or weaken the temporal claim without evidence."

**Verdict: ADDRESSED** (design counterbalances the concern; the residual
per-subgroup effect is disclosed, not claimed eliminated).

## What the manuscript already documents

Methods §2.2: "The temporal type has a known directional response preference;
its two nuisance factors are counterbalanced 24/24 against the gold in an
exact 2×2×2 design, so the preference nets to chance."

Results §3.6 (VAL-1), [R37]-[R38]:

> "The temporal 2×2×2 nuisance design is exactly balanced. At the control
> anchors, answered-valid accuracy differs by nuisance subgroup (0.849 vs
> 0.992 by gold rank; 0.868 vs 1.000 by gold list position) — a documented
> directional response preference that the counterbalancing nets to chance
> (pooled control answered-valid accuracy 0.922). Its raw strict accuracy is
> abstention-dominated, so its H1 rank is read with the answered-valid
> sensitivity."

## Does this already cover "answer position may influence response strategy"?

**Yes, exactly.** "Gold list position" (whether the correct entity is
listed first or second in the question) is precisely the "answer position"
effect the teammate raises, and it is:

1. **Measured, not assumed away:** the manuscript reports the actual
   subgroup gap (0.868 vs 1.000 answered-valid accuracy — a real, sizeable,
   disclosed effect at the individual-subgroup level).
2. **Controlled by design, not by claim:** the 2×2×2 factorial balances this
   factor 24/24 against which side holds the gold answer, so the position
   effect cannot systematically inflate or deflate the *pooled* estimate used
   in H1/H3.
3. **Correctly scoped:** the manuscript says the preference "nets to chance"
   at the pooled level — it does **not** say the position bias is eliminated,
   removed, or absent for any individual item or subgroup. This is the
   accurate claim: counterbalancing controls for a nuisance factor in the
   aggregate; it does not erase the underlying model behaviour that produced
   it.
4. **Propagated to H1:** the manuscript explicitly instructs that temporal's
   H1 rank should be read together with the answered-valid sensitivity
   (because raw strict accuracy for temporal is abstention-dominated, not
   because of the position effect per se) — Methods §2.2, Results [R38],
   Discussion §4.6.

## Checking for over- or under-claiming

- **Not overclaimed:** no sentence says the temporal benchmark is "unbiased,"
  "free of positional effects," or "corrects for" the preference — only that
  the counterbalancing "nets to chance" for the pooled statistic, which is the
  literal, evidenced consequence of a balanced factorial design over a
  measured per-subgroup bias.
- **Not underclaimed:** the manuscript does not hide or minimise the bias — it
  states the numbers (0.868 vs 1.000; 0.849 vs 0.992) plainly, which is a
  larger and more specific disclosure than many papers give a counterbalanced
  nuisance factor.
- **Appendix depth:** finer detail (per-seed, per-item subgroup breakdowns)
  already exists in Appendix A-VAL, referenced from Results.

## No new evidence gathered or needed

This feedback item asks whether the *existing* design and disclosure are
sufficient — it does not present new data. Per instruction, no new analysis
was run and the temporal claim's strength was neither increased nor decreased.

## Classification

**addressed** (per the four-way scale in the task: addressed / partially
addressed / unresolved-but-disclosed / genuine blocker). The specific concern
raised — an answer-position effect — is (a) already measured and reported with
exact numbers, (b) already controlled for in the aggregate by the pre-registered
2×2×2 design, and (c) already correctly scoped in the prose ("nets to chance,"
not "eliminated"). No edit made; none needed.
